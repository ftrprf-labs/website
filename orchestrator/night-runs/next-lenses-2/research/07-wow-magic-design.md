# The Experience Moment of a Lens (Wow & Magic Design)

**EPIC-2 WS-7 (MAC-114) — analysis/read-only**

> Experience design for the top Maculis lenses. This is a design/strategy artifact. It changes no product code and does not claim knowledge of any private repo. It builds on `04-website-presentation.md` (the restrained blur-to-sharp micro-reveal, "lens = perspective, not verdict") and `01-first-five-next-lenses.md` (the evidence-bound, fail-closed lens contract). Assumptions are marked `[ASSUMPTION]`. All sample reveal copy respects the house rule: **no hyphen or dash used as a stylistic pause in visible copy.**

## 0. What we are designing (and what we refuse to design)

We are not designing outputs. We are designing a **moment**: the half second where a person's private expectation about their own site meets one piece of evidence they had not looked at, and the two do not match. That mismatch is the whole product. Everything else is packaging.

The design target for every lens below is a single arc:

1. **Sees** — the first thing on screen, before any thinking happens.
2. **Expects** — the prior the person already carries (usually flattering to themselves).
3. **Discovers** — the one grounded thing Maculis surfaces from public, verifiable evidence.
4. **The gap** — the "he?" (confusion) that turns into "hm!" (recognition). This is the load bearing beat. No gap, no lens.
5. **Reveal** — how it lands. One qualitative sentence. Never a number, never raw data.
6. **Remembers tomorrow** — the durable residue. If nothing survives the night, it was a demo, not a lens.
7. **Shows someone** — why they paste the link into a chat. "Pass the Lens" shareability.

**Two failure modes we reject up front:**

- **The dashboard reflex.** Answering a lens with a panel of tiles is the death of the moment. A lens that resolves into a grid has stopped being a perspective and become a verdict. If a design keeps reaching for tiles, the lens question was too broad.
- **The gimmick.** A reveal that is delightful once and empty forever. The test is section 6: strip the animation, strip the novelty, and ask whether the person changes one thing on Monday. If not, it is theatre.

The rest of this document holds each lens to that bar, and says plainly where a lens fails it.

## 1. First Five (existing) — the journey lens

**Sees.** A single frame of their own site, held still for a beat, slightly soft, the way you see something across a room before your eyes adjust. Not a report. A pause.

**Expects.** "I know my own homepage." The tester has looked at this site a thousand times. Their prior is total familiarity. That familiarity is exactly the blind spot: they no longer see it, they remember it.

**Discovers.** The First Five reframes the site as *first contact*: what actually lands in the opening moments for someone who has never met them. The journey walks First Impression into Reveal into Recognition, and the Recognition gate ("Herken je dit") is the pivot. Maculis surfaces one grounded observation about that first meeting.

**The gap.** The "he?" is small and physical: the site the tester remembers and the site a stranger meets are not the same site. The "hm!" is the Recognition gate landing. When the tester says "yes, I recognise this," they are agreeing that a blind spot was theirs, not the tool's. That consent is the magic. Maculis did not grade them. It held up a mirror at the right angle and waited.

**Reveal (sample, ships in NL per doc 01).**
> When someone meets your site for the first time, one idea is meant to arrive before the others. We looked at the opening view to see whether it does.

**Remembers tomorrow.** Not a finding. A *habit of doubt*: "I should look at my own front door as if I had never walked through it." That reframe outlives any single observation, which is why First Five is the anchor lens and not a gimmick.

**Shows someone.** The tester shows a colleague not the result but the *experience*: "watch what it does with the first five seconds." The share is the reframe, not the score, which is precisely why it survives being passed on. There is no number to argue with.

**Critical note.** First Five's durability comes entirely from the Recognition gate. If the gate is ever softened into an auto advance (finding pushed at the tester without the "do you recognise this" beat), it collapses into an audit and loses its whole claim. The gate is not UX polish. It is the product.

## 2. The Finance Lens

**Sees.** A quiet card: "What does it cost to work with you?"

**Expects.** The owner assumes the answer is obvious on their site. "Of course our pricing is clear, it's right there." Nearly everyone believes their commercial path is legible because they built it and they know where everything is.

**Discovers.** Maculis follows the path a real buyer walks from the homepage toward "what will this cost me," using only public, verifiable pages. It observes whether that path arrives anywhere, or dead ends at "contact us for a quote," or fractures into inconsistent numbers across pages.

**The gap.** The "he?" is the owner watching the walk stall. The "hm!" is realising the wall they built on purpose ("we qualify leads by making them ask") is indistinguishable, from the outside, from a wall that means "we are hiding something" or "we do not know our own price." The evidence is neutral. The discomfort is the owner's.

**Reveal (sample).**
> A visitor who wants to know what you charge reaches a point where the page stops answering and asks them to get in touch instead. We followed that path from your homepage.

**Remembers tomorrow.** A specific, actionable doubt: "our pricing page is a shrug." That is durable because it maps to a decision the owner controls.

**Shows someone.** Founders forward this to a co founder or a board member as a settled argument: "see, I told you the pricing wall costs us." Pass the Lens works here because the evidence is external and un gameable.

**CRITICAL STANCE — overclaim flag, load bearing.** "Finance Lens" is a dangerous name. A website surface contains almost no legitimate signal about a company's *financial health*, and any lens that implies it can read solvency, revenue, or runway from public pages is a fraud we must refuse to build. The only defensible Finance lens reads **pricing legibility**: can a buyer find out what it costs, and is that number consistent. That is real, evidence-bound, and useful. Everything beyond it ("this company looks financially unstable") is unsupported by the evidence and must fail closed. Recommend renaming to **Pricing Clarity** so the name cannot write a check the evidence will not cash.

## 3. The Marketing Lens

**Sees.** A card: "Who is your site talking to?"

**Expects.** "We speak to our ideal customer." The team has a persona doc. Their prior is that the site is aimed, focused, addressed to someone specific.

**Discovers.** Maculis reads the loudest public signals on the primary view (the largest heading, the primary call to action, the first thing the eye is directed to) and observes *who those signals actually address*. Not sentiment, not tone analysis. Just: what does the page put first, and is it one audience or four.

**The gap.** The "he?" is seeing that the biggest, boldest element on the page speaks to a different person than the team believes they serve, or speaks to everyone at once, which is no one. The "hm!" is the quiet recognition that the homepage is a committee compromise wearing a persona's clothes.

**Reveal (sample).**
> The most prominent message on your page invites a broad audience to do several things at once. We looked at what the page puts first and largest.

**Remembers tomorrow.** "Our homepage is trying to please the whole meeting." Durable, because it names a political truth the team already half knew.

**Shows someone.** This is a *meeting weapon*. A marketer pastes the lens to end a circular argument about the hero section. It travels because it is neutral testimony from outside the org's politics.

**CRITICAL STANCE — boundary and overlap.** A Marketing lens is one careless step from "brand sentiment analysis," which is interpretation dressed as fact and must be refused (precision over recall). It also overlaps heavily with First Five's Clarity of Promise (doc 01, Lens A). The honest separation: Clarity asks *is the promise present and plain*; Marketing asks *is the page aimed at one audience or diffused across many*. If that line cannot be held cleanly in practice, fold Marketing into Clarity rather than ship two lenses that answer the same question with different chrome. Two lenses pointing at one gap is padding, and padding reads as a thin product.

## 4. The Reputation / Findability Lens

**Sees.** The person's own link, sitting in a mock chat bubble, the way it would look pasted to a friend. Nothing else.

**Expects.** "My link looks fine." Almost no one has ever looked at their own link preview. The prior here is not confidence, it is *never having thought about it at all*, which makes the gap sharp.

**Discovers.** Maculis renders what the link actually becomes when it leaves the site: the share preview (title, description, image) built from public head tags, and how the site presents itself to being found and forwarded. This is doc 01's Findability Basics (Lens E) turned into an experience instead of a checklist.

**The gap.** The "he?" is visceral and immediate: the preview shows a broken image, or last year's tagline, or a raw URL where a title should be, or a photo cropped to a meaningless corner. The "hm!" is realising that *this*, not the homepage, is the first thing most people ever see of them, because links travel further than visits.

**Reveal (sample).**
> When your link is shared, it arrives without the preview card most people expect, so it shows as a bare address instead of your name and message. We looked at how your page presents itself to being shared.

**Remembers tomorrow.** Strongest of the slate. "Every link I have ever sent looked like that." The residue is retroactive and slightly painful, which makes it stick and makes it get fixed.

**Shows someone.** This is the most naturally shareable lens in the whole set, because the artifact *is* a shareable object. The person pastes their own link into a chat to show a teammate the broken card. Pass the Lens is not a feature bolted on here. It is the native shape of the lens.

**Critical note.** Highest durability, lowest interpretation risk, cleanest evidence. The only trap is scope creep into "SEO score," which reintroduces the number we reject. Keep it to *the shareable preview and the plain findability facts*, each one a thing the person can see for themselves. Do not let it grow a ranking.

## 5. Surprising lens one — "The Dead End" (the page you never designed)

**Sees.** A card that simply says: "This is a page your visitors reach too."

**Expects.** Nothing. The owner has no prior about this page because they have never seen it. Everyone designs the pages they *want* people to land on. Almost no one designs the page people hit when a link rots or a URL is mistyped.

**Discovers.** Maculis fetches the site's own error and empty states (the 404, the page behind a dead internal link) and surfaces what a lost visitor actually meets there. Often it is a blank wall, a server default, or a message that offers no way back.

**The gap.** The "he?" is meeting a page on your own site you did not know existed and did not write. The "hm!" is realising that the visitors who see this page are, by definition, the ones already confused, and the site answers their confusion with silence or a shrug.

**Reveal (sample).**
> When a visitor follows a link that no longer works, your site shows them a page with no way back to you. We looked at where broken links lead.

**Remembers tomorrow.** "There is a room in my house I have never walked into, and my most lost visitors are standing in it." Durable because it is a small, fixable, and slightly haunting realisation.

**Shows someone.** Shared for the surprise and the empathy: "look what happens to people when a link breaks." It travels on the uncanny feeling of a page you own but never saw.

**Critical note.** Genuinely durable and cheap, low interpretation, clean evidence. The risk is that a well built site returns a perfectly good 404, in which case the lens must fail closed and say nothing, per doc 01 discipline. A lens that manufactures concern about a good error page to justify its own existence is a dark pattern. Silence on a clean subject is the correct, and confident, outcome.

## 6. Surprising lens two — "The Squint" (what survives the blur)

**Sees.** Their homepage, deliberately blurred almost to abstraction, the way you see a page across a room or through tired eyes. Then it does *not* resolve. It stays blurred, and asks: "What can you still make out?"

**Expects.** The owner expects their key message to punch through the blur, because in their mind it is the loudest thing on the page.

**Discovers.** By holding the blur, Maculis surfaces what actually dominates the visual hierarchy when detail is stripped away: often a stock hero image, a cookie banner, or a navigation bar, while the actual message dissolves into grey.

**The gap.** The "he?" is that the thing which survives the squint is not the message, it is the furniture. The "hm!" is realising that a hurried or distracted visitor, which is most of them, sees roughly the blurred version, and the message they were counting on never arrives.

**Reveal (sample).**
> When your page is reduced to shapes and weight, the element that stands out most is not your message but the space around it. We looked at what holds the eye before any reading happens.

**Remembers tomorrow — HONEST DOUBT.** This is the beautiful demo I am most suspicious of, and the brief asks me to say so plainly. The blur to sharp motion is Maculis's signature, so "The Squint" is almost too on brand: it *demos* spectacularly and photographs well for a landing page. But interrogate the residue. Visual hierarchy is genuinely interpretive, which sits uneasily with precision over recall and with fail closed. Worse, the "insight" ("your hero image outweighs your text") is one a designer half knows and a non designer may not be able to act on. There is a real risk the person says "huh, neat" and changes nothing on Monday. That is a gimmick's signature: high delight, low residue.

**Verdict.** Ship it only if it can be pinned to a **concrete, verifiable** claim ("the largest element by area carries no message and is decorative") rather than a soft aesthetic read ("your page feels cluttered"). The former is evidence-bound and can fail closed on a clean page. The latter is vibes, and vibes are exactly what a restrained, evidence-bound product must refuse, however good they look in a hero animation. If it cannot be pinned to concrete evidence, keep The Squint as a *presentation metaphor for the whole product* (doc 04's blur to sharp) and do **not** promote it to a standalone lens. A metaphor does not have to fail closed. A lens does.

## 7. Cross cutting: the reveal copy discipline

Every reveal above obeys the same three rules, and they are worth stating because they are what keeps this from becoming a dashboard:

1. **One sentence of consequence, one sentence of evidence.** "Here is what a visitor meets" plus "here is where we looked." Nothing more. The second sentence is the privacy and trust move: it shows the person exactly what was read, so the observation is verifiable and the product never looks like it knows more than it saw.
2. **No number, ever, in the reveal.** Note a real tension with doc 01, whose Dutch samples count things ("Vier afbeeldingen", "het jaartal 2021"). For the public wow moment this brief holds a stricter line: **no count in the reveal sentence.** A count invites the visitor to treat the lens as a score with one data point, which reopens the exact door we shut. Counts, if ever needed, belong in the verifiable detail a person opens on purpose, not in the landing line. This stricter rule should be reconciled with the in product journey copy. `[ASSUMPTION]` the public reveal and the in journey reveal may legitimately differ in register.
3. **Fail closed is a first class reveal.** On a clean subject the correct output is a calm "nothing to flag here," never a manufactured concern. A lens that always finds something is a lens that finds nothing, because it has stopped being evidence-bound. Silence is a feature, and on the website it should read as confidence, not as an empty state.

## 8. Durability verdict (the honest scorecard, in words not numbers)

| Lens | The moment | Durable tomorrow? | Native shareability | Chief risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First Five | Meeting your own front door as a stranger | Strong (a habit of doubt) | High (share the reframe) | Softening the Recognition gate into an audit |
| Finance / Pricing Clarity | The pricing wall you built, seen from outside | Strong (maps to a decision) | High (settles arguments) | The name overclaims financial health; rename |
| Marketing | Your homepage is a committee compromise | Medium to strong | High (meeting weapon) | Overlaps Clarity; fold in if the line blurs |
| Reputation / Findability | Your own link, broken, in a chat bubble | Strongest (retroactive sting) | Highest (the artifact is the share) | Scope creep into an SEO score |
| The Dead End | A page you own but never saw | Strong (small, fixable, haunting) | High (empathy and surprise) | Must stay silent on a clean 404 |
| The Squint | What survives when detail is stripped | **Doubtful** (delight over residue) | High as a demo, thin as a lens | Interpretive, may not fail closed; keep as metaphor unless pinned to concrete evidence |

**Reading of the table.** The lenses that endure share one trait: the discovery maps to a decision the person controls and can verify without us. Findability, Pricing Clarity, and The Dead End are the strongest because their evidence is external and un arguable. First Five endures on a different axis, as a reusable reframe rather than a single finding. The Squint is the cautionary case the brief asked for: a gorgeous demo whose durable value is unproven, and which should stay a presentation metaphor until it can be tied to a concrete, fail closable claim.

3. Can visual hierarchy (The Squint) be reduced to a concrete, fail closable claim, or does it stay a metaphor? This decides whether it is a lens at all.
